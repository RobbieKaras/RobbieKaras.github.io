---
title: Why I keep a dev log
date: 2026-08-01
tags: [meta]
---

I started keeping a per-project log because I kept forgetting why I'd done
things.

Not *what* — the code is right there, and git tells me what changed. The part
that disappears is the reasoning. Why did I pick this approach over the other
one? What did I try first that didn't work? A month later, in an interview or a
code review or just explaining my own project to someone, "what" is easy and
"why" is gone.

So the format here is two sections. **What changed** is just facts — files
touched, what got added or removed. I can generate that part mostly from memory
or the diff. **Why I made this change** is the part I write myself, in my own
words, at the end of the session while it's still fresh. Even when the answer is
"I don't really have a good reason, it just seemed simpler," that's worth
writing down, because next time I'll know it wasn't a considered decision.

The point isn't to produce polished writing. It's to make myself actually
articulate the reasoning once, so I still have it later.
